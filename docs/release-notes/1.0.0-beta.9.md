# Gusset – Pattern Drafter 1.0.0 beta 9

Gusset is a free program for drafting sewing patterns in 2D, with a 3D window where you can sew the patterns together and try them on an avatar. Everything runs on your own computer, and your patterns stay there.

This beta brings almost everything that is planned for 1.0, and **its file format is the one 1.0 will read**: what you make now will open in 1.0. **Everything you can see with the default settings works**; tools that are not ready yet are hidden. It is still a test version, so **please save your work often**, and tell us what you find.

**The easiest way to get it is the website:** [gussetdrafter.com](https://gussetdrafter.com/download) offers the right file for your computer, and you can also try Gusset in your browser with nothing to install.

## The first start

The builds are not signed yet, so Windows and macOS warn you the first time. [Install help](https://gussetdrafter.com/help) shows what to click, with pictures:

- **Windows:** in "Windows protected your PC", click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. The installer installs for you alone and asks for no administrator rights.
- **Mac:** open the `.dmg` and drag Gusset to Applications. At the first start macOS says it cannot check the app: click **Done**, then open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security and click **Open Anyway** near the bottom. If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal: `xattr -cr "/Applications/Gusset – Pattern Drafter.app"`
- **Linux:** make the AppImage executable (its Properties ▸ Permissions, or `chmod +x` on the file), then double-click it. On Ubuntu 22.04 or newer you may need `libfuse2` (`libfuse2t64` on 24.04).

At the first start a welcome box shows a few things to try. Help ▸ Welcome… brings it back.

## Your files

- **The file format is now frozen.** Files you save in beta 9 will open in Gusset 1.0, unchanged. Later versions may add new settings, but never change or remove the ones that are there.
- **Files from every earlier beta open in beta 9** and are upgraded when you save them. **Keep a copy of an important file saved by your old beta**: once saved in beta 9 it no longer opens in beta 8 or older.
- **Your settings, library and unsaved work stay where they were.**
- **Beta 1 to beta 8 tell you about this version** when they start, with an **Update available** button at the top right. You can also choose Help ▸ Check for Updates…. The 0.9.0 betas do not: download this version once by hand. If you imported project files with a 0.9.0 beta, their grainlines may be turned by 90°: choose **File ▸ Import ▸ Re-read Grainlines from Original File…** and pick the original `.zprj` or `.zpac`.

## What's new since beta 8

### Files and formats

- **File ▸ Save As ▸ Project (with Other Files)…** saves one `.zip`: the project, and beside it every file it uses as files of their own (pictures, light profiles, avatar accessories, your own avatar). **File ▸ Open** reads such a `.zip` back. This is the easiest way to send a project to someone.
- **Save As ▸ Garment…, Garment (Selected)… and Pattern…** save the garment alone (no avatar, lights or history); Pattern… saves the patterns in 2D only. **Open ▸ / Add ▸ Garment… and Pattern…** read such files.
- **Save As / Open** for **Render Properties**, **Camera Projection**, **Camera Transformation**, **User Setting**, **Hand Pose**, **Avatar** and **Tool Set**; **Save As ▸ Surface Textures…** saves every picture the project uses as one `.zip`.
- **Add ▸ Avatar…** replaces the avatar in the scene as one undo step: Undo brings the old one back with its pose, size and accessories. If the avatar has edits of its own, a box asks first.
- **A file from a newer version is protected.** When you open a file that a later version of Gusset saved with settings this version doesn't know, a message says so. A plain Save then asks once before it writes over that file, and offers Save As to keep the original. Settings this version doesn't know are written back unchanged where it can (they stay attached to their pattern, seam or fabric).
- **File ▸ Import ▸ OBJ, FBX and glTF 2.0**: bring in an avatar, an avatar accessory, or a button, buttonhole or zipper slider.
- **File ▸ Export ▸ FBX and USD**: the garment as it is draped, a mesh for each pattern with its texture places and colour, at true size.
- **The OBJ export's texture coordinates are now 0 to 1**, the OBJ standard, instead of millimetres.
- **Much more of a `.zprj` / `.zpac` file comes in** (File ▸ Import ▸ Import .zprj / .zpac…): each fabric's colours, weight, thickness and friction, how its stiffness differs between weft, warp and bias, and its front texture; topstitches with their stitch length, thread and rows; buttons and buttonholes where they were; zippers, as a zipper between the two edges they join. A binding comes in as a binding you can change; piping comes in as the patterns and seams the file builds it from. The import report names what was left out.

### Sewing and the 3D window

- **Auto Sewing** (3D toolbar, 3D ▸ Sewing) sews the lines it is sure of and marks the ones it left. Arrange the patterns around the avatar first.
- **Fold 3D Garment**: lay a finished garment flat on the floor and fold it, for showing it. After Lay Flat on an avatar whose arms hang down, one button puts the avatar in a T-pose, lets the garment settle again and lays it flat.
- **Smart Arrangement**, **Symmetric Arrangement**, **Reverse-Arrange** and **Roll Up** (a cuff or a rolled hem) on the 3D pattern menu; **Copy Symmetric Weights**.
- **Mesh Type Quad**: a pattern's cloth as a square grid along its grain line, for a softer fold.
- **Press**: Pressed Open (the default) holds a seam flat; Pressed to One Side folds the two layers against each other.
- **Lacing** and **Edit Lacing**: a cord threaded through eyelets, on the front and the back in turn; a shorter cord pulls them together when you simulate.
- **Pulling the cloth while simulating behaves like pulling real clothes**: a garment slides and turns where you pull it and stays there; stretchy fabric stretches far, and nothing passes through the body or comes off it. **Pull Strength** is in Settings ▸ 3D ▸ Simulation.
- **Seam Taping** is on the 3D toolbar too.
- **Binding and piping make patterns to cut.** A binding (the 3D toolbar's Binding, or right-click a free outline line ▸ More ▸ Binding…) is a fold-over binding made as **one strip to cut**: twice the finished width plus a seam allowance each side (its own, the document's by default), with a fold line along its middle and notches at its quarters and at the matching quarters of the edge. Choose **Bias** for woven fabric or **Cross-grain** for knits; the grainline is drawn to match. **Reduction** cuts the strip shorter than the edge (10 % for cross-grain by default, 0 % for bias) so it hugs the edge, and the notches ease the difference in evenly. **Two-piece binding** makes an outside and an inside strip cut to the edge's own curve instead. A piping (Piping on a seam, or Edit Sewing's right-click ▸ More ▸ Piping…) makes its strip, round the cord with a seam allowance each side, on the bias or cross-grain. The strips are cut again when you change the edge or the box, go when you delete the binding or piping, and print, nest and export (DXF too) with their seam allowance, fold line, grainline and notches.

### The avatar

- **Avatar accessories**: a hat, shoe, belt or hair that follows the avatar and that the cloth collides with. Make one from cloth (Convert ▸ to Avatar Accessory) or bring in a mesh (Avatar ▸ Register Accessory…).
- **Lines on the avatar's skin** (3D Pen (Avatar)), **free sketching** (3D Pencil (Avatar)), **Flatten** (a shape drawn on the avatar becomes a pattern), **avatar tapes** and **Attach to Measure** (a waistband held onto the waist).
- **The avatar's Property Editor** (click it with Select/Move): Item No., the body's friction, its colour. **Move to Ground / Move to Center**.
- **Pose presets are undo steps and are saved with the project**; **Register Pose…** keeps the avatar's pose in your list.

### Materials, colours and UV

- **Colorways**: the same garment in other colours, side by side in the Colorway Editor (Production ▸ Colorway, O), in snapshots, the render, the print and the BOM.
- **The UV Editor**: where each pattern lies in the texture's square, with Auto UV Packing, a UV Snapshot and Bake Textures.
- **Pattern Matching**: stripes and checks matched across seams; Lock in Place; flip a texture on one pattern; Edit ▸ Context Menu ▸ Materials has the grain and texture rows.

### Render

- **Lights**: an **IES light** with a photometric profile, and the **Dome** lit by a panorama picture (also high dynamic range `.hdr` and `.exr`), turned, locked to the camera, or laid on the floor round the avatar.
- **Camera**: Panorama, and a Physical Camera with depth of field.
- **Crop**, **HSV Exponential**, **Linear Workflow**, and the schematic saved as **SVG**; an internal line's Show Schematic Line, Z-Offset and Face.
- **File Path** (desktop app): choose a folder once, and every render is saved there without asking.

### The library and the workspace

- **The Library is a panel** (the bookshelf at the top right, Y): garments, avatars, poses, fabrics and trims as tiles with pictures; your own folders and items; drag items out onto the windows.
- **Register Button / Buttonhole / OBJ**: a mesh as a button, buttonhole or zipper slider.
- **Customize Tools**: regroup, reorder and hide the toolbars' tools.
- **More display switches**, **Graphic Options** and **Default Files** in Settings.
- **Print Layout's Number of Garment**: several garments, or sizes, on one marker.

### Fixed

- The schematic's seam lines are clean (a back view's centre seam is one line).
- Changing anything in Settings ▸ User Settings no longer resets settings the box does not show.
- A Spot light now lights a Path Traced picture, and a Dome lit by a picture alone is the only light of a Quick one.
- **On a computer without working 3D graphics** (WebGL: an old computer, a virtual machine, a blocked graphics driver) Gusset now starts at once instead of after 30 seconds. The 2D window works as usual; the 3D window says why it stays empty.

## Known issues

- **Files saved in beta 9 don't open in beta 8 or older.** Keep a copy saved by your old beta if you need to go back.
- **While you pull the cloth, a long stretched edge can cut across the avatar for a moment.** It comes back round once you let go.
- **Sending to a plotter directly is hidden** until it has been tried on a real plotter. File ▸ Export ▸ Plotter (PLT) writes the file.
- **Auto 3D Arrangement is not available yet.** Place patterns on the arrangement points by hand.
- **The Stress Map's N/m and kPa and the Pressure Map are relative values**, from the simulation's own forces.
- **A fabric's Back and Side** keep their Type and Texture Mapping, but these are not drawn yet.
- **Changing an early step does not yet update the later steps** of the history.
- **How the cloth hung is kept only at Save States** and a few other places. Going to any other step says "This step's cloth was not kept: Re-drape or Simulate".
- **The simulation is a proof of concept.** It shows roughly how a garment hangs. It is not accurate enough to judge fit.
- **The path tracer gets ready again at every start**, about 20 seconds on a mid-range graphics card. Without a graphics card, Final Render uses Quick.
- **In a browser**, Path Traced, a Particle Distance under 10 mm and pictures over 4096 pixels are the desktop app's only, as before.
- **The fabric presets, the size run and parts of the skirt block are placeholder values,** to be replaced by real ones.
- **DXF output has not been tried on a cutter.** Printing true to size depends on printing at 100 %: measure the 50 mm square on the first sheet.
- **The project-file import** is known for files written in 2026 only. It leaves out the avatar, trims such as stoppers, zipper sliders and pullers, and a fabric's damping and buckling. **An imported fabric's overall stiffness is one of Gusset's fabric presets**; only how it differs between weft, warp and bias comes from the file.
- **Binding and piping strips are not simulated as cloth in 3D.** They are kept out of the 3D garment; the binding and the piping's cord are simulated along the edge and the seam, and drawn there.
- **Exporting to other 3D programs** writes the garment only, without the avatar or its motion.
